# Investigation 22-B: selected-slot candidate scan

`006ceb90` is a complete **local static** contract for one slot selector directly called by R21-B `006c50d0`. Its 2,899 original PE code bytes at raw file offset `0x2ceb90` decode as 832 x86 instructions, all address/mnemonic/operand matched to a read-only Ghidra 12.1.4 export from independently restored checkpoint 21. The pinned executable SHA-256 is `0c78e7da6d0bcb4603fa179449bbe6d1b6b5e1c627a22c445d14d489bd9bff86`. All instruction addresses below are virtual addresses. This is **not** an original-instruction execution oracle or a complete selection/lifecycle reconstruction.

The [site evidence](../evidence/match-selection-22-sites.json) retains 166 local branch sites, an eight-entry external switch-table digest, 47 direct call sites to 15 distinct targets, three direct bounded-RNG sites, 13 `__ftol` sites, four direct nonframe write sites, 212 explicit memory-read sites, 35 explicit memory-write sites including frame scratch, and 64 x87 instruction sites. The one normal RET is at `006cf5f8`; two diagnostic tails follow it in the same native function body. The eight switch-table dwords at virtual `006cf6e4` are pinned PE **data outside** the 2,899-byte code body, not incorrectly counted as instructions. Their targets are all within the decoded body. Every decoded instruction is structurally reachable when the two `00945934(-1)` calls are treated as terminal diagnostic boundaries. If those calls return, no normal stack/return contract is asserted for that continuation.

## ABI and candidate order

Entry `ECX=m` is the match-like receiver. The three dword stack slots have low byte `side`, low byte `mode`, and full pointer `mask`, respectively. The body reads the first two as bytes, not as normalized dword arguments. It sign-extends `side` and forms candidate `m+0x4796+(20*s8(side)+i)*0x1be` for `i=0..19`, in ascending order. `RET 12` cleans the three slots. EBX, EBP, ESI and EDI are saved/restored on the normal return; incoming EDX is overwritten. No null/range guard validates `m`, `side`, array bounds or nested pointers. EAX returns the chosen candidate pointer or zero on the admitted mask-present no-candidate path. It is not a boolean.

For each candidate, the local tests occur in this order:

1. When low8(mode)==5 and `mask!=0`, a nonzero `mask[20*s8(side)+i]` skips the candidate. Only this mode reads the mask. A signed-negative byte at candidate `+0x19` also skips it.
2. Side0/side1 candidates equal to `dword(m+0xf59e)` / `dword(m+0xf5a2)` respectively pass a mode/coordinate exclusion gate. Modes 1, 2, 3 and 8 skip those special candidates. Modes 6/7 can continue only under the signed-byte `m+0x8ea7/0x8ea8` coordinate checks shown by the original branches. Other side bytes do not select either special pointer test.
3. Signed candidate `+0x19 > 10` writes byte `0xff` to that field and skips. A zero candidate `+0x6d` pointer writes the same byte and skips. These are the only two direct candidate-state store sites. Earlier negative status skips without this write.
4. Dispatch uses `s8(mode)-1` with unsigned range check `0..7`, then the pinned eight-entry table. Modes outside 1..8 enter a diagnostic tail. Cases 6 and 7 share one entry; the other six modes have distinct entries. The exact switch target sequence is in the site evidence.

The mode cases produce a candidate score through different local paths. Modes 1/2/8 use `006f09b0` float32 grid loads and x87 arithmetic; mode 3 uses repeated `00897990` comparisons and can draw `R(1000)` at `006ceedc` or `R(500)` at `006cef38` before forming a score. Mode 4 forms an integer score of 100 or 0 from the candidate's indexed word test. Mode 5 has a separate receiver/record eligibility chain (`005fd210`, `0053b900`, `005fd480`), a bounded `00601070` probe loop with a signed-byte counter from zero through at most 49, and a fallback score branch. Its `006cf19b` random call uses a sign-extended candidate-linked byte as the bound; a zero result enters a further x87 score adjustment. Modes 6/7 use a float32 table comparison, `00897990` comparisons and x87 scoring; their alternate branch can use `006f09b0`. Every `00945640` call is the existing x87-to-integer `__ftol` boundary, **not** RNG. Score formulas and full callee behavior remain unvalidated numerically, especially for exceptional x87 inputs and mutable aliases.

Here `R` is the previously mapped bounded RNG `0090cfc0`. Only the three addresses above directly call it; the CFG check proves no normal **single candidate iteration** can reach two of these sites. Thus direct calls occur in candidate-index order, at most one per visit and at most 20 in one normal invocation. A candidate may make none. The three sites are **not** an unconditional three-draw sequence. Every other callee, including grid lookup and the service/diagnostic paths, remains a separately scoped transitive read/write/RNG/failure boundary. The previously mapped `006f09b0` local grid-load body has no direct RNG, but that does not resolve all other callees here.

At the join `006cf5ac`, a first scored candidate is selected regardless of score sign. Otherwise `CMP SI,word ptr [ESP+0x1c]` compares signed low 16-bit scores and replaces the chosen pointer only on strict greater; an equal score retains the earlier index. The score is stored as a dword but only its low word participates in subsequent comparisons. High score bits and `__ftol` overflow therefore cannot be replaced with an ordinary full-width `max` without more evidence. The loop increments `i`, compares it signed to decimal20, and repeats while `i<20`.

After 20 visits, a nonzero chosen pointer returns normally. With no chosen pointer and nonnull mask, it returns zero. With no chosen pointer and null mask, the second diagnostic tail runs; it does not return zero on the locally admitted normal path. Both diagnostics call `00944cff`, `0090d130`, `00944c9f`, `005e8290`, clear global dword `00b67a34`, call `005cc7d0` and `005a8f60`, then pass `-1` to `00945934`. Earlier research traces the latter's termination machinery toward `ExitProcess`, but this local body does not independently prove every diagnostic callee returns or termination under every environment.

## Reproduction and limits

Use Capstone 5.0.7 and run `python scripts/extract_match_selection_22.py PRIVATE_EXE --exports PRIVATE_EXPORTS_22B --preflight PRIVATE_PREFLIGHT_22B --output evidence/match-selection-22-sites.json`. The extractor checks the PE size/SHA, export manifest and file hashes, exact-old native preflight, every complete original/native instruction and operand, all local branch destinations and the external switch table before opening the output. `scripts/check_match_selection_22_integrity.py` repeats normal and `python -O` extraction and rejects a changed specimen or changed native operand in both modes without output. The [validation receipt](../evidence/match-selection-22-validation.json) records those checks. Node24 research checks pass separately; they validate earlier units, not this selector's execution.

The body gives no live pointer validity, candidate array lifetime, callee side-effect stability, connected match outcome, x87 control word or full score equivalence. A called routine can alter later candidate fields, receiver state or RNG. Frame aliases and exceptional termination/return are outside this static contract. The 20-candidate and at-most-one-local-draw facts concern original local control flow, not guaranteed whole-match RNG consumption.
